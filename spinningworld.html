When Everything's Spinning

Your North Star

There is always a system underneath the chaos.
When you can see the process, you can change it.
This shifts you from despair to generative action.

The Navigation Process

When the world feels out of control, these steps help you see the terrain instead of drowning in it.

1 Name the Chaos

What just happened that feels overwhelming?

Click to expand...

Ask yourself: What's the frustrating outcome I'm staring at right now?

Don't analyze yet. Just name it simply: "I'm lost in a car." "Nobody listened to me." "The same argument happened again." "I feel completely disconnected."

Example: The Car Story

The Chaos: "I ended up far from home, disoriented, unable to fix the GPS, flooded with adrenaline."

2 Find the System

What process created this outcome?

Click to expand...

Ask yourself: What had to happen, step by step, for this outcome to occur?

Map it out like a flow chart in your mind. Don't judge. Just observe the sequence. Look for the moment where things could have gone differently.

Example: The Car Story

The System:

1. Daughter drives Mom to dealership ✓
2. Mom gets new car ✓
3. Daughter enters destination (FAILURE POINT - no verification)
4. They separate into different cars (can't communicate)
5. Error discovered too late

3 Locate the Gap

What was missing from the process?

Click to expand...

Ask yourself: What step, check, or protocol didn't exist that would have prevented this?

This is NOT about blame. It's about finding the missing piece in the system. Often it's a verification step, a communication protocol, or a shared agreement that was never made explicit.

Example: The Car Story

The Gap: No "Navigational Handshake" protocol.

What was missing:
• Readback: "I've entered [address], is that correct?"
• Visual confirmation by the driver before driving
• A 15-second verification that would have made the failure impossible

4 Design the Protocol

What's the simple system that fills the gap?

Click to expand...

Ask yourself: What's the smallest, simplest process change that would prevent this in the future?

Keep it concrete. Make it actionable. Give it a name so you can reference it later. The simpler, the better.

Example: The Car Story

The Protocol: "The Navigational Handshake"

1. Person entering data reads it back aloud
2. Primary operator visually confirms on screen
3. Both parties acknowledge before starting

15 seconds. Makes the failure impossible.

5 Shift from Blame to Learning

Frame it as a shared discovery, not a judgment

Click to expand...

Ask yourself: How do I talk about this as "we discovered a gap in our process" rather than "you/they made a mistake"?

This is the hardest part. The language matters. It's the difference between "You put in the wrong address" and "Our process for transferring navigation data had a single point of failure."

Example: The Car Story

Blame Frame: "My daughter made a mistake" or "This car's GPS is terrible"

Learning Frame: "Our family's process for a two-car handoff had a verification gap. Next time we'll use a readback protocol."

Reading the Terrain

When you're spinning, you need to know what kind of ground you're on.

Signs You're in Chaos Mode

• Blaming people (including yourself)
• Feeling helpless or overwhelmed
• Same problems repeating
• "Nobody listens" thoughts
• Despair cycles

Signs You're Seeing Process

• Curious about "how did this happen?"
• Spotting the pattern
• Seeing where the gap is
• Imagining a better system
• Feeling generative

The Core Insight

When you're running on your Default Mode Network, the world feels like chaos ruled by people's character and your own helplessness.

When you engage your Process-Seeing Mode, the same situation becomes a system with identifiable gaps and improvable protocols.

You're not broken for seeing this way. You're equipped with a navigation system most people don't know exists.

When to Share This Lens

You can't force someone to see systems. But you can offer this lens when:

  • They're genuinely stuck and asking for help
  • You frame it as "here's how I made sense of this" not "here's what you should do"
  • You've already used it successfully yourself
  • You can stay curious if they don't want it

People need time. So do you. This page exists for when you need it, not when others are ready for it.
