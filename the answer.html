When Someone Asks:

"What do you want from me?"

Instead of being shocked, here is an answer that speaks to universal human needs. This is a guide to transform a moment of conflict into a chance for connection.

A First Response

Pause, take a breath, and reframe the conversation from demand to connection.

"I think what I want is what we both probably want. To feel heard, to be understood, and to know that we can count on each other. I don't want anything from you, but I would like to build something with you: a little more understanding and a little less pain."

The Universal Human Needs

Connection & Belonging

The need to feel part of something larger than ourselves, to know we are not alone.

  • Acceptance & Empathy
  • Love, Intimacy & Trust
  • Community & Consideration

Safety & Trust

The need for emotional and psychological security, not just physical safety.

  • Security & Stability
  • Honesty & Authenticity
  • Reliability & Predictability

Understanding & Recognition

The fundamental need to be seen for who we are and to feel that we matter.

  • To Be Seen & Heard
  • Respect & Acknowledgment
  • Clarity & Validation

Autonomy & Agency

The need to be the author of our own lives and make our own choices.

  • Choice & Freedom
  • Independence & Dignity
  • Space to be Oneself

Meaning & Growth

The need to feel our lives have purpose and that we are developing our potential.

  • Purpose & Contribution
  • Learning & Evolving
  • Effectiveness

The Highest Goals of This Approach

Mutual Understanding

The goal is not agreement, but for each person to feel truly understood. "I see your perspective and your needs."

Co-Creation of Solutions

To shift from a win-lose battle to a win-win partnership. The question becomes: "How can we find a way that works for both of us?"

Re-establishing Connection

To use this moment not as a sign of failure, but as an opportunity to build a deeper, more honest, and resilient bond.

Use this moment as an invitation for shared humanity.
